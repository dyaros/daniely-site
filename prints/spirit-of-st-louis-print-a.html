The story

A piece of aviation history

“Each click of the shutter felt like a tribute to the daring spirit of Charles Lindbergh.”

As I stepped into the Missouri History Museum at Forest Park in St. Louis, I felt a rush of excitement coursing through me. I was on a quest to capture the spirit of adventure embodied by the iconic Spirit of St. Louis airplane — this replica, a faithful tribute to the original aircraft flown by Charles Lindbergh, held a special place in my heart.

The Spirit of St. Louis carried the name of the city whose financial backers helped make Lindbergh's historic flight possible. Lindbergh's historic solo flight across the Atlantic in 1927 captivated the world and cemented St. Louis's place in aviation history. This replica had made its own mark too, appearing in the film The Spirit of St. Louis, starring Jimmy Stewart.

My personal connection to this legendary plane began years ago, when I first saw it hanging proudly in Lambert Airport. Its sleek silhouette against the backdrop of the bustling terminal left an indelible impression on me. Standing before the replica in the museum, camera in hand, I felt a surge of nostalgia mixed with anticipation as I began to capture its essence — the gleaming metal, the sturdy wings that once carried dreams across continents, and the intangible spirit of adventure that seemed to linger in the air.

In that moment, I felt a profound connection to history, to the city of St. Louis, and to the timeless allure of flight.

A St. Louis story

A connection to St. Louis

Since Charles Lindbergh's historic solo flight across the Atlantic in 1927, the Spirit of St. Louis has been inseparable from the city's aviation heritage — named in honor of the St. Louis financial backers who funded the flight itself.

This photograph was taken at the Missouri History Museum in Forest Park, not far from Lambert Airport, where the photographer first encountered the aircraft years ago. It's a piece made in St. Louis, about St. Louis, for anyone who feels that history too.

About the photographer

Daniel Yaroschevsky

St. Louis, Missouri

Daniel Yaroschevsky is a Saint Louis–based photographer working across architectural, fine-art, and portrait photography. His work is grounded in patience — waiting for the right light, the right angle, the detail most people walk past — and in a lasting interest in the stories objects and places carry with them.

The Spirit of St. Louis is a personal project born from that interest: a lifelong fascination with the aircraft that became an enduring part of this city's aviation history, photographed where it now hangs, at the Missouri History Museum in Forest Park.
